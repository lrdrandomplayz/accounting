## Chapter 8: Irrecoverable Debts and Allowance for Receivables

### Section A: Multiple choice (1 mark each)

**8.1** Which double entry writes off an irrecoverable debt?

* A. Debit trade receivable, credit irrecoverable debts
* B. Debit irrecoverable debts, credit trade receivable
* C. Debit bank, credit irrecoverable debts
* D. Debit allowance for irrecoverable debts, credit bank

**8.2** Which concept is the main reason for creating an allowance for irrecoverable debts?

* A. Going concern
* B. Prudence
* C. Consistency
* D. Business entity

**8.3** Trade receivables are $30,000. The allowance is to be 4%. The allowance brought forward is $1,000. What is the effect on the Statement of Profit or Loss?

* A. Expense of $1,200
* B. Expense of $200
* C. Income of $200
* D. Expense of $1,000

**8.4** A debt of $400 written off last year is received in full this year. What is the effect on this year's profit?

* A. Decrease by $400
* B. No effect
* C. Increase by $400
* D. Increase by $800

**8.5** Where is the allowance for irrecoverable debts shown in the Statement of Financial Position?

* A. Under current liabilities
* B. Added to trade receivables
* C. Deducted from trade receivables under current assets
* D. Under non-current liabilities

### Section B: Short answers

**8.6** Explain two differences between an irrecoverable debt and an allowance for irrecoverable debts. (4)

**8.7** On 1 March 2025, Tomas, a credit customer who owes $750, is declared bankrupt. Prepare the journal entry, with a narrative, to write off the debt. (3)

### Section C: Structured question

**8.8** Grace keeps an allowance for irrecoverable debts of 3% of trade receivables. Her year end is 31 December. Trade receivables at each year end were:

| Year | Trade receivables before write-offs $ | Debts to be written off $ |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 40,000 | 0 |
| 2024 | 50,000 | 0 |
| 2025 | 36,000 | 1,000 |

There was no allowance before 2023.

a) Calculate the allowance required at the end of each year. (3)

b) State the amount charged or credited to the Statement of Profit or Loss each year, and whether it is an expense or income. (3)

c) Prepare the Allowance for irrecoverable debts account for the three years. (6)

d) Show the Statement of Financial Position extract for trade receivables at 31 December 2025. (2)

## Chapter 10: Tangible Non-current Assets and Depreciation

### Section A: Multiple choice (1 mark each)

**10.1** Which is capital expenditure?

* A. Fuel for a delivery van
* B. Buying a delivery van
* C. Repairing a delivery van
* D. Insuring a delivery van

**10.2** A machine costs $25,000. Its residual value is $5,000 and its useful life is 5 years. What is the annual straight line depreciation?

* A. $4,000
* B. $5,000
* C. $6,000
* D. $20,000

**10.3** Equipment costs $50,000. It is depreciated at 20% per year, reducing balance. What is the depreciation charge in Year 2?

* A. $10,000
* B. $8,000
* C. $6,400
* D. $20,000

**10.4** A vehicle cost $15,000. Accumulated depreciation is $9,000. It is sold for $5,000. What is the result?

* A. Profit of $1,000
* B. Loss of $1,000
* C. Loss of $10,000
* D. Profit of $5,000

**10.5** Which is **not** a cause of depreciation?

* A. Wear and tear
* B. Obsolescence
* C. A rise in the market price of the asset
* D. Passage of time

### Section B: Short answers

**10.6** Give two reasons why a business charges depreciation. Name the concept linked to each. (4)

**10.7** Loose tools were valued at $2,000 at the start of the year. Tools costing $800 were bought during the year. At the year end the tools were valued at $2,100. Calculate depreciation for the year. (2)

**10.8** State which depreciation method suits a motor vehicle and explain why. (3)

### Section C: Structured question

**10.9** On 1 January 2023 Musa bought a machine for $40,000. It is depreciated at 25% per year, reducing balance. The year end is 31 December.

a) Calculate the depreciation for 2023, 2024 and 2025. (3)

b) Show the NBV of the machine at 31 December 2025. (1)

c) On 1 January 2026 the machine was sold for $15,000 by cheque. Prepare the Machinery disposal account. (5)

d) State where the result of the disposal appears in the financial statements. (1)

**10.10** A machine is bought for $12,000 on 1 April 2025. Depreciation is 10% per year on cost, straight line, calculated monthly. The year end is 31 December 2025. Calculate the depreciation for 2025. (2)
