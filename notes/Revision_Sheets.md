# Revision Sheets

One page per topic for quick revision before a test. All amounts are in Malaysian Ringgit (RM). Full explanations are in each chapter's notes.

## Chapter 8: Irrecoverable Debts and Allowance for Receivables

**Key formulas**

* Allowance required = trade receivables (after write-offs) x percentage
* Change in allowance = allowance required - allowance brought forward
* Net trade receivables (SOFP) = trade receivables - allowance

**Double entry at a glance**

| Transaction | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Write off an irrecoverable debt | Irrecoverable debts | Customer (trade receivable) |
| Reinstate a debt written off earlier | Customer | Irrecoverable debts recovered |
| Cash from a debt written off earlier | Bank | Customer |
| Create or increase the allowance | Income statement | Allowance for irrecoverable debts |
| Decrease the allowance | Allowance for irrecoverable debts | Income statement |
| Transfer irrecoverable debts at year end | Income statement | Irrecoverable debts |

**Where does it go?**

| Item | Statement | Heading |
|---|---|---|
| Irrecoverable debts | SPL | Expenses |
| Irrecoverable debts recovered | SPL | Other income |
| Increase in allowance | SPL | Expenses |
| Decrease in allowance | SPL | Other income |
| Allowance (balance) | SOFP | Deducted from trade receivables, current assets |

**Top 5 mistakes**

1. Charging the whole allowance to the SPL every year. Only the change goes to the SPL.
2. Working out the percentage before writing off the irrecoverable debts.
3. Adding the allowance to trade receivables instead of subtracting it.
4. Forgetting that a decrease in the allowance is income.
5. Recording a recovered debt only as cash, without reinstating the customer's account first.

## Chapter 10: Tangible Non-current Assets and Depreciation

**Key formulas**

* Straight line: (cost - residual value) / useful life
* Reducing balance: carrying amount at start of year x rate
* Revaluation: opening value + purchases - closing value
* Part year (monthly basis): annual depreciation x months owned / 12
* Carrying amount = cost - accumulated depreciation
* Profit or loss on disposal = sale proceeds - carrying amount

**Double entry at a glance**

| Transaction | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Buy a non-current asset by cheque | Asset (for example, Machinery) | Bank |
| Depreciation for the year | Income statement | Provision for depreciation |
| Disposal: transfer cost | Disposal | Asset |
| Disposal: transfer depreciation | Provision for depreciation | Disposal |
| Disposal: proceeds | Bank | Disposal |
| Loss on disposal | Income statement | Disposal |
| Profit on disposal | Disposal | Income statement |

**Where does it go?**

| Item | Statement | Heading |
|---|---|---|
| Depreciation for the year | SPL | Expenses |
| Loss on disposal | SPL | Expenses |
| Profit on disposal | SPL | Other income |
| Cost, accumulated depreciation, carrying amount | SOFP | Non-current assets |

**Top 5 mistakes**

1. Using cost instead of the carrying amount for reducing balance.
2. Forgetting to deduct residual value in straight line.
3. Recording depreciation in the asset account instead of the provision account.
4. Treating repairs as capital expenditure.
5. Putting a profit on disposal on the wrong side of the disposal account. A profit balances on the debit side.

## Chapter 11: Accruals and Prepayments

**Key formulas**

* Expense = paid - opening accrual + opening prepayment + closing accrual - closing prepayment
* Income = received - opening accrued income + opening income in advance + closing accrued income - closing income in advance

**Double entry at a glance (year-end adjustments)**

| Adjustment | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense | Expense account | Accrued expenses (other payables) |
| Prepaid expense | Prepaid expenses (other receivables) | Expense account |
| Accrued income | Accrued income (other receivables) | Income account |
| Income received in advance | Income account | Income received in advance (other payables) |
| Unused stationery | Inventory of stationery (other receivables) | Stationery |

**Where does it go?**

| Item | SPL | SOFP |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense | Add to the expense | Current liability |
| Prepaid expense | Subtract from the expense | Current asset |
| Accrued income | Add to the income | Current asset |
| Income received in advance | Subtract from the income | Current liability |

**Memory trick:** if the business owes money, it is a liability. If the business is owed something, money or a service, it is an asset.

**Ledger account in 4 steps**

| Step | What to enter | Expense account (rent, insurance, wages) | Income account (rent received, commission) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Step 1: Start of year (Balance b/d)** | Last year's closing balance, dated the first day of the year | Owing: **credit** side. Prepaid: **debit** side | Due: **debit** side. In advance: **credit** side |
| **Step 2: Bank** | Cash paid or received during the year | **Debit** side | **Credit** side |
| **Step 3: Profit or Loss** | This year's expense or income, worked out with the formula | **Credit** side | **Debit** side |
| **Step 4: End of year (Balance c/d)** | This year's closing balance, dated the last day of the year | Owing: **debit** side. Prepaid: **credit** side | Due: **credit** side. In advance: **debit** side |

Total both sides after Step 4, then bring the balance down on the opposite side for next year.

**Top 5 mistakes**

1. Putting an accrued expense under current assets.
2. Counting the months wrongly. Write the months out if you are unsure.
3. Using the cash paid figure in the SPL without adjusting.
4. Putting the balance c/d on the wrong side of the ledger account.
5. Forgetting the opening accrual or prepayment brought forward from last year.

## Chapter 12: Fundamental Accounting Principles and Concepts

| Concept | One line meaning | Typical example |
|---|---|---|
| Business entity | Business and owner are separate | Owner's private spending is drawings |
| Duality | Every transaction has two sides | Debit and credit of equal value |
| Money measurement | Record only what has a money value | Staff skill is not recorded |
| Historical cost | Record assets at cost | Land stays at the price paid |
| Going concern | The business will continue | Assets at cost less depreciation |
| Realisation | Revenue when earned | Credit sale recorded on delivery |
| Accruals | Match income and expenses to the period | Accruals, prepayments, depreciation |
| Consistency | Same methods every year | Same depreciation method |
| Prudence | Do not overstate assets or profit | Allowance; inventory at lower of cost and NRV |
| Materiality | Small items treated simply | A stapler is an expense |

**Qualities of useful information:** relevance, reliability, comparability, understandability.

**Inventory:** value at the lower of cost and net realisable value (selling price - costs to sell or repair).

**Top 5 mistakes**

1. Mixing up realisation (when to record revenue) and accruals (matching to a period).
2. Saying prudence means understating profit on purpose. It means do not overstate.
3. Naming a concept without explaining how it applies to the situation.
4. Valuing inventory at selling price instead of the lower of cost and NRV.
5. Forgetting that going concern is the reason assets are not shown at closing-down values.

## Statement of Profit or Loss and Statement of Financial Position

**Key formulas**

* Net revenue = revenue - sales returns
* Cost of sales = opening inventory + purchases - purchases returns + carriage inwards - closing inventory
* Gross profit = net revenue - cost of sales
* Profit for the year = gross profit + other income - expenses
* Working capital = current assets - current liabilities
* Capital at end of year = capital at start + profit - drawings = net assets

**Closing entries at a glance**

| Entry | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Transfer revenue | Revenue | Income statement |
| Transfer opening inventory and purchases | Income statement | Inventory, Purchases |
| Record closing inventory | Inventory | Income statement |
| Transfer expenses | Income statement | Each expense account |
| Transfer profit | Income statement | Capital |
| Transfer drawings | Capital | Drawings |

**Where does it go?**

| Item | Where |
|---|---|
| Carriage inwards | Cost of sales |
| Carriage outwards | Expenses |
| Discount received | Other income |
| Discount allowed | Expenses |
| Drawings | Deducted from capital in the SOFP |
| Bank overdraft | Current liabilities |
| Loan repayable after 12 months | Non-current liabilities |

**Top 5 mistakes**

1. Putting carriage inwards under expenses.
2. Using the trial balance allowance instead of the new allowance in the SOFP.
3. Treating drawings as an expense.
4. Forgetting loan interest owing.
5. Using each trial balance item more than once, or missing one. Tick each item as you use it.
