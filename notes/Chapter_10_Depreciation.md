# Chapter 10: Tangible Non-current Assets and Depreciation

>
> All amounts are in Malaysian Ringgit (RM).

## 1. Learning goals

1. Tell the difference between non-current assets and current assets.
2. Tell the difference between capital expenditure and revenue expenditure.
3. Explain what depreciation is and why it is charged.
4. Calculate depreciation using the straight line, reducing balance and revaluation methods.
5. Record depreciation in the ledger and in the financial statements.
6. Record the sale (disposal) of a non-current asset and find the profit or loss.

## 2. Key words

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Non-current asset | An asset bought to **use** in the business for **more than one year**, not to resell. Example: premises, machinery, vehicles. |
| Tangible | You can touch it. Example: a van. (Intangible: goodwill, a brand.) |
| Cost | Purchase price **plus** all costs to get the asset ready for use: delivery, installation, legal fees, first registration. |
| Depreciation | The part of the cost of a non-current asset used up in one year. It is an **expense**. |
| Residual value | The amount you expect to sell the asset for at the end of its useful life. Also called scrap value. |
| Useful life | The number of years the business expects to use the asset. |
| Provision for depreciation | Also called accumulated depreciation. The **total** depreciation charged on an asset since it was bought. |
| Carrying amount | Cost minus accumulated depreciation. Also called net book value (NBV). |
| Disposal | Selling or scrapping a non-current asset. |

## 3. Capital and revenue expenditure

| Capital expenditure | Revenue expenditure |
|---|---|
| Buying or improving a non-current asset | Day to day running costs |
| Benefit lasts more than one year | Benefit used up within the year |
| Shown in the **Statement of Financial Position** | Shown as an expense in the **Statement of Profit or Loss** |
| Examples: buying a van, delivery and installation of a machine, building an extension | Examples: fuel, repairs, insurance, wages, road tax |

**Why it matters:** if you record capital expenditure as an expense, profit is **understated** and assets are **understated**. If you record revenue expenditure as an asset, profit and assets are **overstated**.

Capital **receipts** (for example, money from selling an old van, or a loan) are not revenue. Revenue **receipts** (sales, rent received, commission) go to the Statement of Profit or Loss.

## 4. Why do assets lose value?

Four causes of depreciation:

1. **Wear and tear:** the asset is used and wears out (a delivery van).
2. **Passage of time:** a lease runs out after a fixed number of years.
3. **Obsolescence:** the asset becomes out of date because newer models exist (old computers).
4. **Depletion:** the asset is used up (a mine or quarry).

## 5. Why charge depreciation?

* **Accruals (matching) concept:** the cost of the asset is spread over the years it helps earn revenue.
* **Prudence:** assets are not overstated in the Statement of Financial Position.
* It gives a true figure for profit each year.

Remember: depreciation is **not** cash. No money leaves the bank when you charge depreciation.

## 6. Method 1: Straight line

The same amount is charged every year.

**Formula:**

> Annual depreciation = (Cost - Residual value) / Useful life in years

Or a fixed percentage of **cost** each year.

### Worked example 10.1

A machine costs RM20,000. Expected residual value is RM2,000 after 4 years.

Annual depreciation = (20,000 - 2,000) / 4 = **RM4,500 per year**

## 7. Method 2: Reducing balance

A fixed percentage of the **carrying amount** (not cost) is charged each year. The charge is high in early years and falls each year.

**Formula:**

> Depreciation = Carrying amount at start of year x percentage

### Worked example 10.2

Equipment costs RM10,000. Depreciation is 20% per year, reducing balance.

**Best for:** assets which lose more value in the early years and need more repairs later, for example motor vehicles. High depreciation early plus low repairs balances with low depreciation later plus high repairs.

## 8. Method 3: Revaluation

Used for many small, low cost items such as loose tools. It is too slow to depreciate each item.

**Formula:**

> Depreciation = Opening value + Purchases in the year - Closing value (valuation)

## 9. Buying part way through the year

Businesses choose a policy and use it consistently:

* **Full year** charge in the year of purchase and none in the year of sale, **or**
* **Monthly (time) basis:** charge only for the months owned.

Example: a machine costing RM12,000 is bought on 1 April. Depreciation is 10% per year on cost. Year end is 31 December.

Full year charge = 12,000 x 10% = RM1,200. Monthly basis for 9 months = 1,200 x 9/12 = **RM900**.

Always read the question to see which policy to use.

## 11. Disposal of a non-current asset

When an asset is sold, open a **Disposal account**. It works out the profit or loss on sale.

| Step | Debit | Credit | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Transfer the cost | Disposal | Asset account | Original cost |
| 2. Transfer the depreciation | Provision for depreciation | Disposal | Accumulated depreciation to date |
| 3. Record money received | Bank (or Cash) | Disposal | Sale proceeds |
| 4. Balance the account | | | Profit or loss |

* If the **credit** side is bigger, the difference is a **profit** on disposal (income).
* If the **debit** side is bigger, the difference is a **loss** on disposal (expense).

**Quick check:** Profit or loss = Sale proceeds - carrying amount at date of sale.

### Worked example 10.5

A van cost RM30,000. Accumulated depreciation to the date of sale is RM18,000. It is sold for RM10,000 cash paid into the bank.

Carrying amount = 30,000 - 18,000 = 12,000. Proceeds 10,000. **Loss = 2,000.**

## 13. Common mistakes

* Using **cost** instead of the **carrying amount** for reducing balance.
* Forgetting to deduct residual value in straight line.
* Recording depreciation in the asset account instead of the provision account.
* Treating repairs as capital expenditure. Repairs keep an asset working; they do not improve it.
* Mixing up the sides of the disposal account. Profit balances on the **debit** side.

## 14. Quick summary

* Non-current assets are used, not sold, and last more than a year.
* Depreciation spreads cost over useful life (accruals) and avoids overstating assets (prudence).
* Straight line: same amount yearly. Reducing balance: percentage of the carrying amount. Revaluation: opening + purchases - closing.
* Disposal: proceeds minus carrying amount equals profit or loss.
