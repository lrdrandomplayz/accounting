# Chapter 8: Irrecoverable Debts and Allowance for Receivables

> Excel file for this chapter: `excel/Ch08_Irrecoverable_Debts.xlsx`

## 1. Learning goals

By the end of this chapter you should be able to:

1. Explain what an irrecoverable debt is.
2. Write off an irrecoverable debt in the ledger.
3. Record a debt that was written off and later paid (recovered).
4. Explain why a business creates an allowance for irrecoverable debts.
5. Calculate the allowance and the increase or decrease each year.
6. Show irrecoverable debts and the allowance in the Statement of Profit or Loss and the Statement of Financial Position.

## 2. Key words

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trade receivables | Customers who bought goods on credit and still owe the business money. |
| Irrecoverable debt | A debt the business is sure it will **never** collect. Example: the customer is bankrupt or has disappeared. |
| Writing off | Removing the debt from the customer's account and treating the loss as an expense. |
| Debt recovered | A debt written off in the past which the customer later pays. |
| Allowance for irrecoverable debts | An **estimate** of the debts which **might** not be paid in the future. |
| Prudence | Do not overstate assets or profit. Record losses as soon as they are likely. |

## 3. Why do irrecoverable debts happen?

Selling on credit brings more customers, but some customers will not pay.

Common reasons:

* The customer is bankrupt (has no money).
* The customer has died or cannot be found.
* The amount is too small to be worth chasing through the courts.

## 4. Writing off an irrecoverable debt

When you are **certain** a debt will not be paid:

| Step | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Write off the debt | Irrecoverable debts account | Customer's account (trade receivable) |
| At the year end | Statement of Profit or Loss (expense) | Irrecoverable debts account |

### Worked example 8.1

Kofi owes the business $600. On 30 June 2025 Kofi is declared bankrupt. Nothing will be paid.

**Journal**

| Date | Details | Dr $ | Cr $ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 June 2025 | Irrecoverable debts | 600 | |
| | Kofi | | 600 |
| | *Debt written off, customer bankrupt* | | |

**Kofi account**

| Dr | | $ | Cr | | $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Jun | Balance b/d | 600 | 30 Jun | Irrecoverable debts | 600 |

**Irrecoverable debts account**

| Dr | | $ | Cr | | $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 Jun | Kofi | 600 | 31 Dec | Income statement (SPL) | 600 |

**Effect:** profit falls by $600. Trade receivables fall by $600.

## 5. A debt written off is later recovered

Sometimes a customer pays after the debt was written off. Two steps:

| Step | Debit | Credit | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Reinstate the debt | Customer's account | Irrecoverable debts recovered | Keeps a full record of the customer's history. |
| 2. Record the cash | Bank (or Cash) | Customer's account | The customer pays. |

At the year end, **irrecoverable debts recovered** is shown as **other income** in the Statement of Profit or Loss (added to gross profit).

### Worked example 8.2

Last year the business wrote off $300 owed by Lina. This year Lina pays the $300 by cheque.

| Details | Dr $ | Cr $ |
|---|---|---|
| Lina | 300 | |
| Irrecoverable debts recovered | | 300 |
| Bank | 300 | |
| Lina | | 300 |

**Effect:** profit rises by $300. Bank rises by $300.

## 6. Allowance for irrecoverable debts

### 6.1 What is it?

At the year end, the business does not know which customers will fail to pay. It knows from experience that **some** will not pay. So it makes an **estimate** and keeps it in an account called **Allowance for irrecoverable debts**.

* It is usually a **percentage of trade receivables**. Example: 5% of trade receivables.
* Always write off known irrecoverable debts **first**, then calculate the allowance on the balance left.

### 6.2 Why create it?

* **Prudence:** it stops the business showing trade receivables at a value higher than it will collect.
* **Accruals (matching):** the expected loss is charged in the same year as the credit sales that caused it.
* It gives a **true and fair** value of trade receivables in the Statement of Financial Position.

### 6.3 Irrecoverable debt vs allowance

| Irrecoverable debt | Allowance for irrecoverable debts |
|---|---|
| Certain loss | Estimated (possible) loss |
| Linked to a named customer | Not linked to any one customer |
| Customer account is closed (credited) | Customer accounts do not change |
| Full amount is an expense | Only the **increase** (or decrease) goes to SPL |

## 7. Recording the allowance

Only the **change** in the allowance goes to the Statement of Profit or Loss.

| Situation | Debit | Credit | SPL effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creating the allowance (first year) | Statement of Profit or Loss | Allowance for irrecoverable debts | Expense (full amount) |
| Increase in allowance | Statement of Profit or Loss | Allowance for irrecoverable debts | Expense (increase only) |
| Decrease in allowance | Allowance for irrecoverable debts | Statement of Profit or Loss | Income (decrease only) |

**Three-step method**

1. Required allowance = trade receivables (after write-offs) x percentage.
2. Change = required allowance at the end of the year minus the allowance brought forward.
3. Positive change = increase = expense. Negative change = decrease = income.

### Worked example 8.3

A business keeps an allowance of **5%** of trade receivables. Year end is 31 December.

| | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trade receivables (after write-offs) | 20,000 | 26,000 | 18,000 |
| Required allowance (5%) | 1,000 | 1,300 | 900 |
| Allowance brought forward | 0 | 1,000 | 1,300 |
| Change | +1,000 | +300 | (400) |
| SPL treatment | Expense 1,000 | Expense 300 | Income 400 |
| Net trade receivables in SFP | 19,000 | 24,700 | 17,100 |

**Allowance for irrecoverable debts account**

| Dr | | $ | Cr | | $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 Dec 31 | Balance c/d | 1,000 | 2023 Dec 31 | Income statement | 1,000 |
| 2024 Dec 31 | Balance c/d | 1,300 | 2024 Jan 1 | Balance b/d | 1,000 |
| | | | 2024 Dec 31 | Income statement | 300 |
| | | **1,300** | | | **1,300** |
| 2025 Dec 31 | Income statement | 400 | 2025 Jan 1 | Balance b/d | 1,300 |
| 2025 Dec 31 | Balance c/d | 900 | | | |
| | | **1,300** | | | **1,300** |
| | | | 2026 Jan 1 | Balance b/d | 900 |

Note: the allowance has a **credit** balance. It reduces an asset.

## 8. Showing it in the financial statements

**Statement of Profit or Loss (extract), year ended 31 December 2025**

| | $ | $ |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit | | 40,000 |
| Add: Decrease in allowance for irrecoverable debts | | 400 |
| Add: Irrecoverable debts recovered | | 300 |
| | | 40,700 |
| Less expenses: Irrecoverable debts | 600 | |

(If the allowance had **increased**, the increase would be listed under **expenses** instead.)

**Statement of Financial Position (extract) at 31 December 2025**

| Current assets | $ | $ |
|---|---|---|
| Trade receivables | 18,000 | |
| Less: Allowance for irrecoverable debts | 900 | 17,100 |

## 9. Common mistakes

* Charging the **whole** allowance to the SPL every year. Charge only the **change**.
* Calculating the percentage **before** writing off the irrecoverable debts.
* Adding the allowance to receivables. You **subtract** it.
* Forgetting that a decrease in the allowance is **income**.

## 10. Quick summary

* Irrecoverable debt: certain loss. Dr Irrecoverable debts, Cr Customer.
* Recovered debt: reinstate the debt, then record the cash. It is income.
* Allowance: an estimate based on prudence. Only the change hits profit.
* SFP shows trade receivables **less** the allowance under current assets.
